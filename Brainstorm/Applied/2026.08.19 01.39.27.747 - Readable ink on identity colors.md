# Readable ink on identity colors

Kategori: theme and identity color | Efor: S | Rank: 2

## Ne
Add `public static Color InkOn(string backgroundHex)` beside `IdentityPalette` in `MainProject/UI/ThemeConstants/AppColors.cs` — WCAG relative luminance of the background, then white or a near-black plum, whichever wins contrast. The plum must be genuinely dark (around `#1A0F18`): a mid plum like `#3A2436` only reaches 4.13:1 on `#7C7BFF` and would fail the assertion below. Apply it at the four inline-style sites that currently paint over an identity color: the account unread badge (`Mail.razor:66`, `style="background:@account.ColorHex"`) and the three `TagStore.ColorOf` chips (`Mail.razor:246`, `:389`, `:401`), and emit `--account-ink` next to `--account-accent` on `.mail-screen` (`Mail.razor:11-12`) so `.mail-row-thread-count` (`MailCss.razor:1027-1035`) can stop hardcoding `TextOnAccent`. Then drop the fixed `color:` from `.mail-tag-chip` (`MailCss.razor:954`) and `.mail-account-unread` (`MailCss.razor:129`). Expect the visual result to be dark text on every chip, not a mixed set — all eight palette colors lose against white, so this is a uniform change to chip appearance app-wide, not a touch-up of two or three outliers. Verify through a `GET /identity` endpoint in `MainProject/zTests/DebugApi.cs` that returns every `IdentityPalette` entry and every account `ColorHex` with its chosen ink and computed ratio, and assert each is at least 4.5:1; the endpoint calls the same public `InkOn` the UI calls, so nothing in a lower layer is widened for the debug path.

## Neden
`AppTheme.cs:161` defines `TextOnFilledSurface => Colors.White`, and that is what `.mail-account-unread` (`MailCss.razor:129`) and `.mail-tag-chip` (`MailCss.razor:954`) both resolve to while their backgrounds come from `account.ColorHex` and `TagStore.ColorOf`. Measured against white, the whole palette fails 4.5:1: `#FFB86B` 1.70, `#E9B949` 1.83, `#4FB6E8` 2.29, `#B79CEF` 2.33, `#34B27B` 2.69, `#EC6FA9` 2.83, `#F4714A` 2.87, `#7C7BFF` 3.42. `TagStore.TagPalette => AppColors.IdentityPalette`, so account dots and tag chips draw from the same eight, and `AccountWizard`/`AccountSettingsPage` hand them out on normal use — there is no color a user can pick that reads well. One helper fixes account badges, tag chips and thread-count pills at once.
